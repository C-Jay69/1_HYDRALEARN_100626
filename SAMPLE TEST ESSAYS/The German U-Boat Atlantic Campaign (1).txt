The Battle of Stalingrad (AGAIN!!!)
The Battle of Stalingrad was an important battle in World War II. It happened between Germany and the Soviet Union in the city of Stalingrad. The battle was very bad because many soldiers and civilians died. I think Stalingrad was important because Germany lost and the Soviet Union became stronger.
Germany attacked the Soviet Union in 1941, and later German forces went toward Stalingrad. Hitler wanted the city because it had factories and was near the Volga River. Also, the city had Stalin’s name, so it was important for pride. The Germans fought hard to take the city, and there was fighting in streets and buildings.
The Soviet soldiers defended Stalingrad. They did not want to give up the city. The weather was also very cold in winter, which made it difficult for the German army. German soldiers did not have enough food or warm clothing. This caused many problems for them.
In November 1942, the Soviet Union attacked the German army from around the city. This trapped the German Sixth Army. The Germans were surrounded and could not get enough supplies. Hitler told them not to surrender, but eventually the German commander, Friedrich Paulus, surrendered in February 1943.
The Battle of Stalingrad changed the war because Germany was no longer winning as much in the east. After this battle, the Soviet Union started pushing Germany back toward Germany. It showed that the German army could be defeated.
In conclusion, Stalingrad was a terrible battle and an important victory for the Soviet Union. Germany lost many soldiers there, and this made it harder for them to win the war.
